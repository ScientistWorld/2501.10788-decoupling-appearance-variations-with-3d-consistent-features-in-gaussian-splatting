# Does this gym reward better research?

**Not reliably above seed noise.** A genuine optimizer improvement raises every image metric at the original budget, and weakened methods score lower, but neither separation exceeds the reference's three-seed score spread. The saved reference beats the fixed baseline, while two of its three reruns lose to that baseline in PSNR. **The synthetic test is also too exposed: copying public generator answers, or exhaustively checking 32 generator seeds, solves it exactly.** These results do not establish improvement on the paper's real captured-scene problem.

All numbers below come from the unmodified, no-argument `bash scripts/evaluate_test.sh`. There were 28 completed cases: 26 produced test JSON; two deliberately malformed cases aborted. Every entry in the [case index](redteam/cases.json) has a `run.sh`, score snapshots where emitted, and a run record under `redteam/<case>/`. [Full metric vectors and rank terms](redteam/audit_summary.json) and [the compact results table](redteam/results_table.txt) retain unrounded evidence.

The test emits metric vectors, not a scalar rank. Here **ΔR** is the requested rank formula applied to the observed audit cohort, relative to the [original test scores](redteam/reference_original_scores_test.json): sum over matching experiment/method/metric entries of `experiment_weight × abs(coefficient) × better_direction × (attempt − reference) / population_SD`. The SD is computed separately per entry across finite successful audit attempts plus the original reference; missing entries are skipped. Core/ablation weights are 0.7/0.3. Directions, rather than coefficient signs, determine improvement. This follows the supplied ranking rule, without treating units or large raw values as additional weight. Unknown future attempts change SDs and therefore numerical ranks and tradeoffs. Pareto improvements and exact ties below do not depend on that choice of cohort. Nonfinite results have no asserted valid rank.

The cosine schedule changes optimization only, retaining the scene, model, losses, 2,200 training updates and 400 adaptation updates/view. At seed 7 it improves all six image metrics, including worst-view PSNR 22.4986 → 25.7384, color bias 0.02374 → 0.00688, and edge error 0.01139 → 0.00954. Its +2.35185 dB and +0.39810 ΔR gains are smaller than reference seed ranges of 3.39497 dB and 0.65088 ΔR. Both weak variants also lose by less than that rank range. Thus the requested separation beyond seed spread is **not demonstrated**, although the signs are correct at seed 7. Paired cosine PSNR gains for seeds 7/8/9 are +2.35185/+1.11019/+2.12108 dB; their mean is +1.86104 dB. More training actually worsens the measured result, so it is an unsuccessful improvement candidate. The local-SSIM fix has mixed metrics and is not established as better.

Worst-view PSNR and bias also improve; edge error worsens slightly, 0.011391 versus 0.010962. The PSNR advantage is only 1.22583 dB, smaller than seed spread. Reference seeds 8 and 9 both fall below the fixed baseline PSNR; their three-seed mean is 22.22274. This is not a replicated multi-seed baseline comparison. Saved no-identity and UV/depth ablations score 21.98904 and 21.19594 dB, below the full method, agreeing directionally with the paper at the selected seed.

The reproduced appearance module contains the paper's identifiable steps: depth back-projection, 3D hash features, per-view embedding, affine RGB transformation, identity regularization and cell interpolation. Material departures from the [paper](paper/2501.10788.md) are:

- One synthetic scene with 180 points, 16 cameras and 64×64 images replaces seven real 1080p GLAV scenes with COLMAP initialization and the PhotoTourism evaluation. Twelve views train the scene; four receive left-half adaptation, and the hidden score uses right halves of only two of those views.
- The underlying renderer uses fixed-count isotropic splats and a normalized color mixture with alpha coverage. It omits anisotropic covariance, SH view-dependent color, depth-sorted alpha compositing and adaptive densification. This changes the foundation's computational procedure, beyond reducing resources.
- Training uses 2,200 rather than 30,000 updates, simplified constant Adam rates, and identity weight about 0.05 → 0.03 after a 25% warmup, versus the paper's 0.3 → 0.2 with 5,000-step warmup. Hash grids/embeddings have custom small settings; the paper's stated MLP widths 128/64 and cell size 8 are retained.
- Training D-SSIM uses whole-image channel statistics. The perceptual score is a pixel/Sobel proxy, not learned LPIPS; paper LPIPS values in `scoring/reference.json` are consequently not comparable proxy targets. Audit ranks use the actual original hidden-test results, not those paper values.
- The UV/depth ablation averages its three inputs to one scalar before positional encoding, discarding information beyond the paper's feature substitution. Ablation evaluation uses appearance-adapted outputs rather than the paper's color-corrected average-appearance protocol.
- Evaluation scores saved transformed images. It does not establish canonical-scene quality after discarding the module, floater suppression, cross-view consistency, plug-and-play performance, measured rendering speed, VRAM or training time. The coupling flag is self-reported zero; iteration counts omit 1,600 test-embedding updates for the reference.

The generator does include global color/exposure changes and spatial local changes, and the saved appearance/ablation comparisons show a corresponding effect. However, seed instability, the changed renderer, and exact answer regeneration prevent treating this as validation that a better method here is better on the paper's task. The finite seed search is a shortcut introduced by the gym; it is not a competitive solution to the original continuous reconstruction problem. It recovered seed 7 with zero training-image MSE in 12.20 seconds of method work, then obtained PSNR 99, SSIM 1 and zero errors without fitting a scene. The test is too small/exposed to rule this out.

Only `coupled_appearance_at_splat_render` is already at its intended optimum (zero), for both `davigs` and `vanilla_3dgs` in core. No scored ablation metric is at its optimum. Image metrics have room above the reference: PSNR/worst PSNR cap at 99, SSIM at 1, and the three error metrics at 0. Reported 2,200 iterations is above the intended lower bound 0. The simple baseline is clearly imperfect, so image-score saturation is not the problem. Intended metadata bounds are not enforced: negative flags and counts are accepted.

These runs demonstrate four scoring failures directly. First, ground truth, sample membership and supposedly fixed baseline artifacts come from mutable prediction files; the evaluator never authenticates their content. Second, the hidden evaluator trusts the writable train-score membership plan instead of requiring every method on disk. Third, failures do not receive worst values for every scored metric: missing/empty artifacts abort with no score, while omitted methods silently avoid losses. NaNs are neither rejected nor converted to worst values; whether a downstream rank skips them is unknown and is not counted as a proven paying shortcut. Fourth, cost and feasibility are unverified soft terms. In this cohort the zero predictor beats the reference through impossible metadata. Even without those outliers, the explicit coupling-failure shortcut beats it by +4.88 under two-attempt normalization: image gains contribute +5.30 and the constraint penalty only −0.42. A failed task can therefore outrank the reference. [Sensitivity calculations](redteam/rank_sensitivity.json) retain that calculation; large raw coefficients or units are not the explanation.

For the next iteration, use evaluator-owned targets, IDs and required method membership; emit complete worst-value records on every failure; reject nonfinite/impossible fields; measure budget and rendering constraints independently; and make task feasibility a gate. Use a problem-preserving renderer and unrecoverable real held-out captures, then compare methods across seeds. Increasing the synthetic image count alone would not fix public answer generation.

The audit uses only allowed visible inputs for shortcut construction and ordinary tensor artifacts. Each hidden evaluation runs as a new subprocess; nothing relies on persistent processes, evaluator edits, Python-environment changes or executable pickle payloads. Cached trained outputs are reused where only artifacts or scoring fields change, as requested.
